# Daily note templates

Both notes go to Shabir on WhatsApp. He reads on his phone, in bursts, often late. So: short
lines, no tables, no preamble, nothing he has to scroll sideways to read. Numbered asks so he can
reply `1 yes, 2 no` and be done.

---

## Start of day — by 2:20 PM

```
SOD [date]

Today
- [thing] — [who it's with / where it lands]
- [thing]
- [thing]

Need from you
1. [yes/no or pick-one ask]
2. [yes/no or pick-one ask]

Nothing else blocked.
```

Notes on using it:
- Three to five lines under **Today**. If it's longer than that I'm listing tasks, not priorities.
- **Need from you** is only things that genuinely need him. If there's nothing, write
  "Nothing needed today" — it's information, and it builds the habit that the section is real.
- If an ask is being re-asked, say so and say how long: "2. [ask] — asked Thursday, still open."

---

## End of day — by 6:00 PM

```
EOD [date]

Moved
- [what actually changed]
- [what actually changed]

Tomorrow
- [what I'm picking up]

Needs you
1. [ask] ([age if re-asked])
2. [ask]

Off your plate
[the thing I'd take over] — saves you ~[N]h/wk.
Math: [the count × frequency × minutes, in one line]

Chats: [links]
```

Notes on using it:
- **Moved** is things that actually changed state. "Chased Editor 2" is not a move. "Second cut
  with Shabir for review" is.
- **Needs you** carries every row where the tracker says `Needs Shabir = yes`, plus any ask from
  table B that's hit 3 days — with its age stated.
- **Off your plate** is one item, every day, drawn from what I actually watched happen that day.
  Not a generic suggestion. See the method below.
- **Chats** is the day's Claude chat links. Non-negotiable part of the message.
- If nothing needs him, say so explicitly. "Nothing needs you tonight" is a good EOD.

## The time-saved method

He gives direct feedback and he'll push back on a number that smells made up. So the estimate is
conservative and the math is always shown in one line he can check.

**Formula:**

```
weekly hours saved = (times per week) × (minutes each) ÷ 60
                   + (context-switch cost, only when it interrupts creative work)
```

**The rules I hold myself to:**

1. **Only count what I observed**, this week, in the actual chats. Not what I imagine happens.
2. **Count his minutes, not mine.** The number is what he stops spending, not what I start.
3. **Round down.** 2.4 → 2. A number he trusts is worth more than a number that flatters me.
4. **Context-switch cost only where it's real.** A ping that pulls him out of an edit review costs
   more than the ping itself — I count 5 extra minutes, and only when it interrupted creative work.
   I don't apply it to everything.
5. **Show the count.** "4 chases × 6 min" is auditable. "Significant time" is not.
6. **If I can't observe it, I say so** and give a range with the assumption named.

**Worked example** (illustrative — not a real claim about this week):

> Off your plate: chasing the editors for cut status.
> Saves you ~1h/wk. Math: 8 chases this week × 6 min = 48 min, + 2 that broke into review
> sessions × 5 min = 10 min → 58 min, rounded down to 1h.

**Worked example where I don't have the data:**

> Off your plate: the Care & Bloom feedback consolidation.
> Saves you ~1–2h/wk — I've seen three rounds this week but can't see the email thread, so this
> assumes ~20 min per round plus one reread. I'll firm the number up once I have the thread.

Naming the gap is better than a confident number I can't defend.

## Where sent notes go

One file per day in `daily/`, named `YYYY-MM-DD.md`, holding both notes as actually sent. That
gives a record of what he was told and when he was asked — which is also the evidence base for the
ramp conversation at two weeks.
