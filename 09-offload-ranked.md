# What I could take off Shabir's plate

Ranked by hours saved him per week. Everything here was observed across the three days — in his two
voice notes, in the live pipeline rows, or in the chats — not imagined.

## How these numbers were made, and how far to trust them

```
weekly hours saved = (times per week) × (minutes each) ÷ 60
                   + context-switch cost, only where it interrupts creative work
```

Five rules I held to:

1. **Only what was observed**, in three days of his actual messages and four live creatives.
2. **His minutes, not mine.** The number is what he stops spending, not what I start.
3. **Round down.** Always.
4. **Context switch only where real** — 5 minutes, and only where a ping broke into review or
   creative work. Not applied to everything.
5. **Where I could not observe frequency, I say so** and give a range with the assumption named.

**The honest health warning:** three days is not a week, and I have not yet counted a single real
chase end to end. Ranks 1–4 rest on observed evidence and I would defend them. Ranks 5–9 rest partly
on his own description of how often something happens, which is a report, not a count. **Every number
below gets replaced with a counted one at the end of week one.** A figure he can check beats a figure
that flatters me.

## The ranked list

*Who has which file, what is due when, who has not replied.* His Day 1 words, and the thing he said
lives in his head.

**Math:** 4 live creatives × ~3 touches/week × 5 min = 60 min. Plus ~5 of those pings landing
mid-review × 5 min context switch = 25 min. That is 1.4h on four cards alone. At a real batch of
five plus the creator side it lands at 2–3h. *Assumption: current volume is below normal, since one
batch is mid-flight.*

**Evidence:** CB-07 — nobody currently knows whether it is live, queued, or never received. That is
a chase he would make himself today.

### 2. Review routing and feedback consolidation — **1.5–2 h/wk**

He is the reviewer for both copy and edit. **The judgment stays his.** What moves is everything
around it: assembling the pack, getting it to him in one piece, consolidating scattered feedback
into one message per round, and re-requesting after a push.

**Math:** ~6 review events/week × 8 min of assembling and chasing = 48 min, plus 2 that arrive in
fragments across threads and need consolidating × 15 min = 30 min → ~1.3h. Range allows for the
rounds the SOP caps at two.

**Evidence:** CB-09 and CB-11 are both waiting on him right now, and neither arrived as a clean pack.

## The first three I would take in week one

Picked for highest hours **at lowest risk**, and — importantly — **nothing blocked on an open
decision**. Taking over a process whose owner is still unknown is how you drop something quietly.

| # | What | Saves | Why this one first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Status chasing + the daily digest** | 2–3 h/wk | Biggest number, zero risk, no gate. Nothing in it requires his judgment, so it needs no permission to start. It is also what makes "he reads instead of chases" literally true. |
| **2** | **Review routing and feedback consolidation** | 1.5–2 h/wk | Second biggest, and it does **not** ask him to delegate judgment — only the assembly around it. That distinction is why it is safe in week one. |
| **3** | **Client review pack prep** | 1 h/wk | Time-critical this week, and prep is the safe half: I build it, he approves it, nothing reaches Care & Bloom without his OK. |

**Week one total: 4.5–6 h/wk**, against a full list of **6.5–9.5 h/wk**.

### What I deliberately left out of week one

- **Creator payments (#5)** — blocked. Who handles it is unreadable in the transcript. Taking over
  a payment process without knowing its owner risks a creator going unpaid while everyone assumes
  someone else has it.
- **Weekly report (#8)** — the weekly review has no day yet. Cannot take over a meeting that does
  not happen.
- **Anything requiring his judgment.** Reviewing scripts and cuts, approving client sends, setting
  dates and rates. Those are not inefficiencies to remove — they are the job only he can do, and
  this whole exercise exists to give him *more* time for them, not to take them away.
