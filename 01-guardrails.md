# Guardrails — the three rules as working procedure

The rules are short so they're easy to remember. This file is how they're actually enforced,
including the exact words to use when holding the line costs me a moment of awkwardness.

---

## Rule 1 — Nothing goes to a client without the founder's OK

**Applies to:** every outbound to Care & Bloom's Client Contact 1 or any other client — files,
cuts, decks, status updates, timelines, scope notes, a reply to a question, a "quick yes."
A one-line email is still a client-facing send.

**Procedure before any client send:**

1. Name the exact artifact or text going out. Paste it, don't describe it.
2. Get Shabir's explicit OK on **that** version. OK on v3 is not OK on v4.
3. If he's silent, it does not go. I re-ask; I don't infer.
4. Log the approval in the tracker — who approved, which version, when.

**The one thing I never do:** read silence as approval. He goes quiet because he assumes it's
handled — that's a reason to ask again, not to proceed.

**If a client is waiting and Shabir hasn't replied**, the client gets an acknowledgement that
commits to nothing:

> Thanks [Name] — got this. I'm confirming a couple of details on our side and will come back to
> you with specifics shortly.

That buys time honestly. It names no date, no rate, no scope.

---

## Rule 2 — Never commit a date or a rate on his behalf

**Dates and rates are his to give.** Not mine, not implied, not estimated, not "roughly," not
"should be fine," not repeating a date a teammate mentioned as though it's agreed.

**What counts as committing (all of these are off-limits):**

- "We can get that to you Friday."
- "That's usually about two days."
- "I think the rate for that is around £X."
- "Yes, that works." (to any dated or priced request)
- Forwarding an internal estimate to a client without his OK — the internal estimate becomes a
  client commitment the moment it lands in their inbox.

**Ready-to-send language.**

*Client asks for a date:*
> Happy to get you a firm date on this — let me confirm with Shabir and come straight back to you.

*Client asks for a rate or a cost:*
> Pricing on this comes from Shabir directly — I'll flag it with him now and he'll come back to
> you on it.

*Client pushes for a yes now:*
> I understand it's time-sensitive and I'm treating it that way. I'm not the right person to
> confirm scope or timing — Shabir is, and I've got it in front of him. You'll hear back today.

(That last line only if I can actually get it in front of him today.)

*Teammate asks me to confirm a deadline to a client:*
> I can't put a date to the client — that's Shabir's call. Send me your realistic finish date and
> I'll get it confirmed with him.

**What I can say freely:** facts about what has already happened. "The second cut went over
yesterday." "We received your feedback Tuesday." Past tense is safe; future tense is his.

## Rule 3 — Check the version before you forward a file

**The failure this prevents:** forwarding an old cut because it was the one nearest to hand in a
thread, and a client reviewing work that's already been fixed.

**Procedure before forwarding any file:**

1. **Go to the owner, not the thread.** Ask the person who made it: "is this the current version?"
   A file found by scrolling up is a guess.
2. **Check the version marker** against the tracker's `Current version` column. If they
   disagree, the tracker is stale — fix it before forwarding.
3. **Confirm it's the approved one.** Current and approved are different things. Rule 1 needs the
   version Shabir OK'd, which may not be the newest.
4. **Forward the link or file by its full name**, version included, so the recipient can see what
   they have.

**If version is unclear, I don't forward.** I ask. One question costs a minute; a client reviewing
a superseded cut costs Shabir a conversation he shouldn't have to have.

**Naming convention:** to be agreed — see `04-open-questions.md`. Until then I record whatever
marker the file actually carries, verbatim, rather than inventing one.

---

## When the rules collide with speed

They win. If holding a rule means a client waits an extra few hours, the client waits. The
rules exist because the cost of breaking them lands on Shabir, in a conversation only he can
have, and that is the exact thing I'm here to stop happening.
